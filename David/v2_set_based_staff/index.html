1 What is a chord? What is a chord progression?

A chord is notes played together

Stack three notes that fit together and you get a chord. C, E and G make C major. Lower the middle note by a half step (E → E♭) and it becomes C minor, which sounds sadder.

A progression is chords in order

Songs move from one chord to the next, usually repeating a short loop. G → D → Em → C is one of the most common, and you will hear it in hundreds of pop and rock songs.

G, D and C are major chords; Em is E minor.

2 Build your own progression

Click a note on the staff, choose ♭, ♮ or ♯ (and major or minor), and the chord lifts above the staff to join your progression (up to 4, in order). Under each note you will see the chord that most often comes next. The songs update in Step 3, right below.

Data is read straight from Erik/chordonomicon_transformed.csv.gz in the Chords-Explorer folder (decompressed in your browser). Seventh, slash, suspended and other extended chords count as their plain major or minor chord; sharps and flats are the same chord (F♯ = G♭); and repeated chords in a row count once, so G–D means G followed directly by D. Each dot is one song, placed by release date, sized by Spotify popularity and colored by main genre; vertical position is random. Songs with no release date or from before 1950 are left out. The year axis tightens to the songs on the chart. The most popular song in each genre is outlined and labeled; selecting a genre outlines and labels its top 5.
